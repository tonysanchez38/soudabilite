Schaeffler diagram calculator: plot a weld and choose the filler metal

The Schaeffler diagram has answered this question since 1949. The calculator uses it, adds dilution and ranks the filler metals. This page explains the calculation on this exact case.

The principle

The Schaeffler diagram predicts the structure of the weld metal from its chemical composition only. Austenite, ferrite, martensite, or a mix of the three.

Each alloying element pushes the metal towards ferrite or towards austenite. The diagram groups the first ones into a chromium equivalent, the second ones into a nickel equivalent, and plots the point on a two-axis chart. The zone where the point falls shows the risk: hot cracking, cold cracking or embrittlement.

Chromium and nickel equivalents

Two numbers are enough to plot a metal. The calculator uses the Schaeffler formulas:

Cr eq = %Cr + %Mo + 1.5 × %Si + 0.5 × %Nb
Ni eq = %Ni + 30 × %C + 0.5 × %Mn

Carbon counts thirty times more than nickel: a few hundredths of a percent of carbon move the point in a visible way. For the S235JR of the materials library, Cr eq = 0.52 and Ni eq = 5.35. For 316L, Cr eq = 21.00 and Ni eq = 13.90.

Dilution

The weld is never made of filler metal alone. It mixes a part of melted base metal A, a part of melted base metal B and the filler metal.

Weld = DA × A + DB × B + DC × C, with DA + DB + DC = 100 %

Dilution is the sum of the two melted parts, DA + DB. It depends on the process and the joint preparation: the calculator uses 15 to 30 % for TIG, 10 to 35 % for covered electrodes, 20 to 40 % for MIG and MAG. The weld point lies on the line between the mix of the base metals and the filler metal.

Worked example: S235JR to 316L with TIG

Butt joint with a V preparation, process 141. The calculator proposes 15 % dilution, split in two equal parts: 7.5 % S235JR, 7.5 % 316L, 85 % filler metal.

Without a suitable filler, the mix of the two base metals falls into the austenite + martensite zone (Cr eq 10.76; Ni eq 9.63). This is the cold cracking zone. The filler metal must pull the weld out of this zone.

309L wire (INERTROD 309L Si)23.10 / 13.0511.6 %Ideal zone over 100 % of the range

308L is right for welding two stainless steels together. With a carbon steel, it does not have enough chromium and nickel to balance the melted steel: the weld moves towards martensite when dilution goes up. 309L has more alloy and keeps the weld in the ideal zone over the whole TIG range. This is why 309L is the reference filler metal for carbon steel to stainless steel joints.

The calculator checks every filler metal of the library that fits the process in the same way, then shows the seven most robust ones.

The three ranking bands

A filler metal is not judged on one point, but over the whole dilution range of the process. The ranking first looks at the worst result, then at the share of the range in the ideal zone.

  1. Ideal zone: 5 to 15 % delta ferrite. Enough ferrite against hot cracking, not enough to make the weld brittle.
  2. Acceptable zone: 0 to 20 % ferrite. To be checked against the service conditions.
  3. Safety corridor: a historical reference inside the A+M+F zone, used when the first two zones cannot be reached.

What the diagram does not tell you

The diagram estimates a trend. It does not measure anything. Here is the frame.

  • The compositions of the library are typical values; the 3.1 material certificate is the reference.
  • The real dilution is measured on a macro-examination of the joint.
  • Nitrogen is not part of the Schaeffler equivalents; the calculator also shows the DeLong equivalents in its summary.
  • Duplex stainless steels are outside the current scope.

The result helps to prepare a preliminary welding procedure specification (pWPS). It is confirmed by the procedure test according to EN ISO 15614-1 and, if needed, by a real ferrite measurement.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Schaeffler diagram used for?

It predicts the structure of the weld metal of a stainless steel, or of a stainless to carbon steel joint, from its composition, and so the cracking risk. It is used to choose the filler metal.

How are the chromium and nickel equivalents calculated?

Cr eq = %Cr + %Mo + 1.5 × %Si + 0.5 × %Nb and Ni eq = %Ni + 30 × %C + 0.5 × %Mn, with the Schaeffler formulas.

Why weld carbon steel to stainless steel with 309L?

Because 309L has enough chromium and nickel to balance the melted carbon steel. On S235JR welded to 316L with TIG, it keeps the weld in the ideal zone over the whole dilution range, where 308L moves towards martensite.

What ferrite content should a stainless steel weld have?

The calculator uses 5 to 15 % ferrite as the ideal zone, and 0 to 20 % as the acceptable zone.
