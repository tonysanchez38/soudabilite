Free online weldability calculator

A carbon steel pipe to weld onto a 304L stainless steel nozzle. The drawing gives no filler metal. The question comes before the arc is struck: what will I get in the weld metal, and will the joint hold?

I built soudabilite.com to answer this question before the first test piece. I work as a technical preparer in industrial piping, and I am a BTS CRCI student. The tool is free, with no account, and every formula refers to its standard.

Open the calculator

What the calculator does

You enter two base metals, a welding process and a joint. The tool chooses the right analysis.

  • Stainless steel or dissimilar joints. It calculates the chromium and nickel equivalents, builds the dilution line, places the joint on the Schaeffler diagram, estimates the ferrite content and ranks 7 filler metals over the full dilution range of the process.
  • Non-alloy and low-alloy steels. It calculates the carbon equivalent and the preheat temperature with the Seferian or BWRA method.
  • Heat input with the thermal efficiency factors of EN 1011-1, shown in kJ/cm (1 kJ/mm = 10 kJ/cm).
  • Export of a preparation sheet, which can be used as a preliminary welding procedure specification (pWPS) according to EN ISO 15607.

The materials library lists 52 base metals and 194 filler metals, with their full chemical composition.

Who it is for

Welding students, welding coordinators, process technicians and technical preparers. Anyone who must choose a filler metal or check a preheat temperature before writing a pWPS.

What it does not do

The tool prepares, it does not qualify. It does not replace a qualified WPS, a welding procedure qualification record (WPQR) or a procedure test according to EN ISO 15614-1. The values it gives are starting points, to check against tests and a macro-examination of the real joint.
